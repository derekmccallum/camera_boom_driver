# Project: camera_boom_driver

## Goal
Motorise a ceiling-mounted camera boom so it swings horizontally around a vertical pole, up to 120 degrees, with ESP32 firmware. Control: two physical buttons (go to 0, go to 120) plus a tablet web UI (go to angle over a set duration, home offset, saved presets, jog, big STOP). A second motor (tilt axis) may be added later, so keep the code axis-generic.

## Mechanical design (decided)
- Boom is balanced; about 50 cm from the pole axis. Camera load roughly 2 kg (a guess).
- 200 mm aluminium lazy-susan ball bearing. Inner ring fixed to the 2020/2040 hanging structure; outer ring rotates with the boom and carries a 6 mm GT2 open belt on its rim, ends anchored on the far side, screw tensioner (no springs: they make the drive elastic).
- NEMA 17 on a fixed plate with a 20T GT2 pulley and two idlers pressing the belt onto the rim.
- Calculated ratio about 16:1, so about 17,000 microsteps per 120 degrees at 16 microsteps. This is an ESTIMATE; steps-per-degree must be calibrated on the real arm and stored as a setting.

## Electronics (decided)
- ESP32 30-pin dev board (CH340, ESP32-D0WD-V3) on a breakout/power board; header jumper set to 3.3 V. Classic ESP32 only (not S3/C3).
- Driver: Adafruit TMC2209 breakout (ADA6121), in use since 2026-11-19 (it replaced the BTT TMC2208 V3.0; the motor runs, user-confirmed). From Adafruit's guide: 5 to 29 V motor supply, up to 2 A set by the pot (UART can override it), VDD 3 to 5 V (wired to ESP32 3V3, not 5V), single-pin UART with auto-baud, SPRD jumper open = StealthChop, no heatsink included. UART: ESP32 RX GPIO16 direct and TX GPIO17 through 1k, both to the board's single UART pin (as on the TMC2208; the resistor was not re-confirmed). Motor pins 1A/1B and 2A/2B. The sketches still use the TMC2208Stepper class. Sense resistors: 0.05 ohm (R1 and R2 on BRA/BRB in Adafruit's Eagle schematic, checked 2026-11-20; the board marking "50" agrees). All sketches 04 to 09 use R_SENSE 0.05 (04 to 08 changed from 0.11 on 2026-11-20 with MOTOR_RMS_CURRENT_MA raised 400 to 740, same real current, about 740 mA, as sketch 08 passed at on this board; sketches 04 to 07 passed earlier on the BTT TMC2208 at about 400 mA real and are untested at 740). Chip version reads 0x21 (sketch 08, user-confirmed). Not recorded: pot setting, MS1/MS2 state and how they set the UART address (a TMC2209 can share one UART between up to 4 drivers; check before adding a second axis on the same UART). The earlier TMC2208 had no UART addressing; its PDN_UART was the 4th header pin counting EN as 1.
- Motor: HANPOSE 17HS4401S (NEMA 17, 40 mm body, 1.8 degree, 5 mm D-shaft). Seller listings disagree on ratings: about 1.5 to 1.7 A per phase, 1.5 to 1.8 ohm, 2.8 to 3.8 mH, 40 to 43 N.cm holding torque. Measured about 2.5 ohm per coil through the harness. Sketch 05's 740 mA is only about half of rated current, so expect roughly half of the holding torque: raise the current for the loaded build (the Adafruit TMC2209 is rated up to 2 A, so stay near the motor's own rating and fit a heatsink). On the Adafruit TMC2209 the motor pins are 1A/1B (one driver coil) and 2A/2B (the other). Wired and working: red 1A, green 1B, blue 2A, black 2B. Both wires of one motor coil must go to the same driver coil. A wrong pairing gives ticking or stuttering without moving (the first attempt on the TMC2209 did this and was fixed; the cause was not recorded). Swapping the two wires of one coil only reverses direction. The old BTT TMC2208 used OA1/OA2/OB1/OB2 with red OA1, green OA2, blue OB1, black OB2.
- Motor supply: 12 V brick (voltage, current and polarity NOT yet confirmed from its label). 100 uF cap across VM/GND at the driver, fuse about 3 A, physical e-stop cutting motor supply only, common ground with the ESP32.
- Pins: STEP 25, DIR 26, EN 27 (active low). Limit sensors GPIO 34 and 35 (input-only). Buttons TBD. Avoid strapping pins 0/2/12/15, flash pins 6-11, and ADC2 while WiFi is on.
- Limit sensors: two BIQU optical endstops (TCST2103), powered at 3.3 V, CW limit on GPIO 34 and CCW limit on GPIO 35 (CW viewed from above). Both are ACTIVE-HIGH as currently wired (pin reads LOW when the beam is clear, HIGH when blocked; sensor LED lit when blocked), measured with sketch 06's `?` command and confirmed by the user. An earlier note that GPIO 34 was active-LOW came from an inference from the sketch 02 LED behaviour and was wrong for the current wiring (the earlier behaviour was never explained, possibly different wiring at the time). Always check raw levels with sketch 06's `?` command if a sensor is swapped or rewired. Polarity is a separate constant per sensor in config.h.

## Software decisions
- Arduino IDE, ESP32 Arduino core (pin the working version), custom firmware. Libraries: FastAccelStepper (step pulses), TMCStepper (UART config).
- WiFi: join the home network as a station (DHCP; the router allocates 10.0.0.100; hostname boom / boom.local), with an own-access-point fallback (BoomCtrl) so the tablet can still connect with no router. Credentials in secrets.h (gitignored). ESP32 is 2.4 GHz only. WebServer for now; move to a WebSocket UI later. Presets/calibration in Preferences. Moves use absolute targets from a homed position (never "move 120 from here").
- Upload: macOS, port /dev/cu.usbserial-8410. Uploads failed at 921600 baud; use 115200.

Sketch 06 limit shuttle (unloaded, 740 mA, bench, not mounted): auto-starts after a 5 s countdown, seeks the CW sensor, measures travel to the CCW sensor, then runs between the two sensors forever at one constant speed (LEG_SPEED_HZ 400 Hz, with acceleration ramps), logging the position error at each hit. User-confirmed the flow of actions is correct. Each sensor stop is an abrupt forceStop, so overshoot past the sensor grows with speed. Passed on hardware (user-confirmed). The position-error figures and the real travel step count have not been recorded here. The "wrong limit" fault is the check for a wrong direction.
Sketch 07 web shuttle (tested on the boom: all features passed, user-confirmed): sketch 06's ping-pong plus WiFi station join (AP fallback) and a web page with a START/STOP button and a live leg-speed slider (100 to 1600 steps/s, default 400, limits in config.h). It does NOT auto-start: it waits for START (page or serial 'g'). STOP decelerates on the ramp and holds with the motor enabled; START resumes without re-homing. The web server runs in its own task on core 0 so page loads cannot delay the limit checks in loop(). Web STOP depends on WiFi, so it is not an e-stop. Live speed change, STOP/resume and the clockwise direction passed on the boom. Not yet confirmed: the AP fallback path.

Sketch 08 passed on it (chip version 0x21). Sketches 04 to 08 are unchanged (TMC2208Stepper class, R_SENSE now 0.05, was 0.11 which was wrong for this board; current set to 740 mA). Not recorded: pot setting.
Sketch 08 web boundaries (tested on the boom with the Adafruit TMC2209: homed and responded correctly to all commands, user-confirmed): sketch 07's web shuttle made self-calibrating. After boot (and WiFi) it counts down 5 s (AUTO_CALIBRATE_ON_BOOT, so the boom moves by itself; STOP or 's' cancels), seeks the FAR optical sensor, measures the travel to the HOME sensor at 400 Hz, and moves to home, END_MARGIN_STEPS (100) inside the home sensor. Home end is HOME_AT_CW_END in config.h (true = CW end). Position 0 = home sensor, counting up away from home; the working area is sensor-to-sensor travel minus END_MARGIN_STEPS at each end, so normal moves end on a smooth ramp and the sensors are only a safety trip (an unexpected trip stops the motor, re-syncs the position and waits; faults stay latched until board reset). Web UI: START/STOP (a run sweeps between the two ends forever with PAUSE_MS at each end; STOP holds and waits, START resumes), live speed slider, RESET (seek the boundaries again, with confirm), HOME (also during a run). START and HOME are disabled until calibrated. Degrees on the page use STEPS_PER_DEGREE = 142.2, an ESTIMATE for display only. MAX_SEEK_STEPS was reduced from 64000 to 30000 (about 210 degrees at the estimated ratio). Serial: g start, s stop, h home, r reset, d disable, ? status. Speed slider limits are unchanged (100 to 1600 steps/s) and current stays 400 mA: not yet tested loaded at higher speed. Not recorded: whether the AP fallback was tried.
Sketch 09 boom v1 (sketches/09_boom_v1, written 2026-11-20, UNTESTED on hardware, only syntax-checked against stub headers; no Arduino toolchain on the dev machine): first production version, built from sketch 08 (same state machine, same sensor and driver handling, TMC2208Stepper class; R_SENSE changed to 0.05 on 2026-11-20, confirmed from the schematic). Changes: (1) SEEK_SPEED_HZ 2000 (was 400; first 1200, raised to 2000 on 2026-11-20, untested); MAX_SEEK_STEPS raised 30000 to 36000 for a 180 degree arm. (2) Run speed range is set in config.h as the time for a 180 degree move including ramps: fastest 2.5 s, slowest 6 s (the default). With the ESTIMATED 142.2 steps/degree that is LEG_SPEED_MAX_HZ 12189 (about 230 rpm of the motor) and LEG_SPEED_MIN_HZ 4571, so the real times scale with the real ratio. Acceleration is speed / RAMP_TIME_S (0.4 s, my choice, not discussed with the user): about 30,000 steps/s2 at the top speed, 3000 at the seek speed. Time for a move of D steps = D / v + RAMP_TIME_S. (3) START sends the boom ONE leg in the current direction (runDir) to the far boundary and holds there (READY); the direction flag then flips and the next START goes back. STOP (as before) decelerates and holds (HALTED), runDir is kept, so START resumes to the same end. HOME sets runDir = away from home; an unexpected sensor trip sets runDir to lead away from the tripped sensor. S_PAUSE and PAUSE_MS removed. Page shows 'N deg in X s' for the slider and the end START travels to. Motor current in sketch 09's config.h was raised to 800 mA for the unloaded boom (untested). With R_SENSE 0.05 that is about 800 mA real, only about 8% above what sketch 08 passed at (about 740 mA real); raise it for more torque. Concerns for the loaded test: 400 mA is far too low for these speeds and ramps (the boom's reflected inertia at the motor is estimated at about 20 kg.cm2, which would need very roughly 10+ N.cm for the fastest ramp, against about 10 N.cm holding at 400 mA and falling with speed), a 12 V supply limits torque at about 230 rpm, StealthChop loses torque at speed (TPWMTHRS / SpreadCycle not configured), and an abrupt sensor stop from 12 kHz may coast past the sensor. Test at the slow end first, raise MOTOR_RMS_CURRENT_MA in steps with a heatsink, and time a real 180 degree move. Added 2026-11-20 (UNTESTED, written by Claude, JS syntax-checked only): a STOP & DISABLE MOTOR button on the page (/disable, state DISABLED): decelerates on the ramp (afterHalt = AH_DISABLE), then the driver is disabled (at once if already at rest; a second press while decelerating disables at once), boundaries forgotten (calibrated = false); from then on only RESET (page or serial 'r') is accepted, and it enables the motor again and re-seeks both sensors. Serial 'd' is unchanged (terminal until board reset).
Next step: record the measured travel steps for a known sensor spacing to calibrate STEPS_PER_DEGREE. Then sketch 10 (09 is now boom v1): absolute moves in degrees from home (go to angle, eased sweep over a set duration), with step-rate limits from the real ratio (about 16:1, roughly 4 kHz for a 4 s 120 degree move at 16 microsteps) once the loaded boom has been tested at that speed and current.

## Safety requirements
Boom swings at head height. Hardware e-stop independent of firmware and WiFi. Soft limits plus a physical stop. Finite moves only; jog must stop if the client heartbeat is lost. Home at low speed. Motor stays disabled until homed.

## Working rules
- Code from the planning chat was written without hardware access and is UNTESTED: treat it as a starting point and flag anything that fails to compile.
- One sketch per folder (Arduino requirement). Put all pins and tunables in one config header.
- Never commit WiFi passwords: use secrets.h (gitignored) with a committed secrets.example.h.
- Ask before assuming where the above says "not verified" or "TBD".
